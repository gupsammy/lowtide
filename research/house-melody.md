# Melodic and organic house: how producers write it, as rules

Research for lowtide's house phase, 26 Sep 2026, from guides, track breakdowns, interviews, theory and two studies. Numbers are starting values for the listening lab. Links after each rule are its sources; the list at the end marks vendor guides, which sell packs or courses.

**The user's artists are slower than the spec.** [SongBPM][sbpm] gives Spotify's tempo estimates for 191 tracks by Jabarov, Azimov and George Kopaliani: median 105 BPM, 69% at 100–116, only 7% in the spec's 118–124, median lengths 3:22–4:59. Estimates can halve or double and names can clash, but all three artists agree. That is organic, downtempo-leaning house, not the club tracks the guides describe.

## Rules worth taking

### Harmony

**H1. Loop few chords, slowly.** 1, 2 or 4 chords, 2–4 bars each, in 8- or 16-bar loops; default two chords of 4 bars. One-chord vamps need moving filters. *Why:* a loop that never resolves can run for minutes. ([Myloops][myc], [Attack/pads][atp], [Chordoo][cho])

**H2. Draw from a minor pool; lean on VI.** i–VI, i–VII, i–VII–VI, i–iv–VII, i–VI–III–VII, v–VI–VII–i. Open phrases on VI, or place it before i. *Why:* VI shares two notes with i, so it lifts without leaving home. ([Myloops][myc], [Hooktheory][ht], [Attack/main-room][amr])

**H3. Give each exotic scale one job.** Harmonic minor: raise the 7th only on V (i–V–VI–V) or only in the lead; sources split, so weight both. Its augmented second (♭6–7) mostly falls. Phrygian dominant (Hijaz): chords I, ♭II, iv, vii; vamp I–♭II. The Andalusian i–♭VII–♭VI–V loops as an ostinato. *Why:* the raised 7th and ♭2 carry the Eastern colour. ([Chordoo][cho], [Myloops][myc], Wikipedia on [harmonic minor][whm], [Phrygian dominant][wpd], [Andalusian cadence][wac])

**H4. Voice low and small; write the top line.** Three notes over 1–1.5 octaves, well below the lead (Myloops: C2–G3 under G4–C6); imply 7ths; allow sus; move least. The top voice is a tune (Am–F–G–Em with top notes C–C–D–B). No chord note a semitone above the bass. Stabs: offbeats, a dotted-8th pulse, or a 16th early, held through beat 1. *Why:* the lead's band stays clear; the top line hooks; early stabs push. ([Myloops][myc], [Ghost][gp], [Attack/scales][aus], [Attack/Chandler][akc])

**H5. Keep one loop all track.** Change its treatment, not its chords: the breakdown reveals it (pad open, full voicing); a later break revoices it. At most once, late, add a new chord. *Why:* the loop holds the trance; late new harmony gives chills. ([Myloops][myc], [Attack/Romance][amro], [Sloboda][slo])

### Melody

**M1. A short motif with air.** Hook under 2 bars; 4–8 notes per 4 bars, with long notes, gaps, and an empty beat before each repeat. Start and end on chord tones; start before beat 1 or answer a chord change on beat 3. Riffs may run a note a beat ("Sweet Melody": 67% chord tones). *Why:* gaps let delay and groove speak. ([Myloops][myc], [Ghost][gp], [Hooktheory][ht])

**M2. Repeat; change only the end.** Play a 2-bar phrase twice, then alter its ending (Melody v2's period form). Over changing chords, use shared notes and octave leaps: Mr Fingers' A–E riff fits Am7, Fmaj7 and Em7. *Why:* repetition builds trance; a new ending holds the ear. ([Myloops][myc], [Attack/ostinatos][aos], [Attack/melody][amel])

**M3. Arps in 16ths.** Chord tones up or up-down; gate 40–60%; decay 100–200 ms; high-pass 250–300 Hz; delay 3/16 or dotted 8th at ~30% feedback; ducked 3 dB to the chords' 6. Open the filter over 16 bars; rest the arp for the drop's first 2 bars. Let some drift against the beat, as in "Rej" (try 3- or 5-step cycles). *Why:* the arp moves while chords stand still. ([Myloops][myc], [EDMProd][emh], [Attack/Rej][arej])

**M4. Widen the range; save the top.** Lead in G4–C6, spanning up to ~16 semitones ("Sweet Melody": A♭3–C5). The top note comes once, by an octave jump at the climax (Böhmer). *Why:* the climax needs room. ([Myloops][myc], [Hooktheory][ht], [PML/Böhmer][pbo])

**M5. Plant, withhold, reveal.** Show the motif early and filtered, then hold it back (Caribou: bars 31 and 129); fragments before the drop; a counter-melody in the third 16-bar block; the theme, or a new one, bare in the breakdown; the full hook at the drop. *Why:* the return feels planned. ([Attack/Caribou][acar], [Ghost][gp], [EDMProd][emh])

**M6. Let parts take turns.** Pair an acoustic-style lead with a soft synth pluck, high stabs with low ones, bass with chord stabs: where one plays, the other rests; swap every 1–2 bars. *Why:* call and response keeps a busy groove clear. ([Melodigging][mdo], [PML/Innellea][pinn], [Attack/Chandler][akc], [Attack/Caribou][acar])

**M7. Follow Hijaz grammar.** Step within a 3–5-note fragment (a jins), then shift up. The 4th degree is a second home: open phrases end there, closed ones on the tonic. Feature the ♭2–3 leap; start on the 5th; slide into key notes; add grace notes. Optionally, Turkish style: degree 3 ~25 cents flat falling, degree 6 ~50 flat in passing. Double the lead an octave down on a soft pluck. *Why:* a maqam is a way of moving, not just a scale. ([Ableton][abm], [taqs.im][taq], [Soundjuce][sj])

**M8. Place the moving moments.** An appoggiatura (an off-chord note on a strong beat, stepping to a chord tone) on the hook's key beat; a phrase repeated a step lower in the breakdown; new harmony and sudden texture change only at break and drop; slight detune and muffling. *Why:* Sloboda linked appoggiaturas and sequences to tears, new harmony and sudden change to chills; Yotto finds muffled, detuned sounds richer. ([Sloboda][slo], [Yotto][yot])

### Bass

**B1. Pick one of five families per track.** Offbeat: each "and"; saw, closing filter envelope, dotted-8th delay. Rolling: 16ths 2–4 of each beat, 65–350 Hz. Root rhythm: root with the kicks, a flourish at the bar's end (Lane 8), or root–octave 8ths (Moroder). Sustained fifth (Enamour). Pedal: one riff under changing chords (Kerri Chandler). *Why:* each clears the kick its own way. ([Attack/bass][abs], [Attack/rolling][arb], [PML/bass][pbl], [Attack/Chandler][akc])

**B2. Plan the pitches.** ~70% root, with a fifth in bar 3 and a walk down in bar 4 (`AAAA AAAA AAEE AAGF`). Skip an offbeat before a chord change; ghost a 16th at velocity 60–70 before the next kick; alternate velocities 100 and 85, mapped to cutoff; notes a 16th long, 80–180 ms release; sometimes move before the chord. *Why:* the root anchors; phrase ends carry the harmony. ([Myloops/bass][myb], [Ghost][gp], [Attack/bass][abs])

**B3. Duck under a tuned kick.** A short kick tuned to the key, body at 50–60 Hz; the bass speaks at 80–110 Hz. Sidechain from a silent quarter-note trigger: 4:1, attack under 1 ms, release 70–140 ms at 122 BPM (scale with tempo), 4–10 dB on bass, 1–3 dB on pads. Organic guides duck lightly, to breathe rather than pump. *Why:* ducking clears the low end and makes the pump. ([Ghost][gp], [Myloops/bass][myb], [Melodigging][mdo])

### Groove

**G1. Set tempo by taste, not genre.** Guides: melodic 120–124, organic 110–124, deep 120–128. Set this station to 104–116, like the user's artists; keep 120–124 for an Anjunadeep-style one. *Why:* match the user's taste. ([SongBPM][sbpm], [Ghost][gp], [BLOND:ISH][bl], [Melodigging][mdo])

**G2. Keep the kit steady.** Short kick every beat; clap on 2 and 4, or one reverb-soaked clap per 2 bars with an early rim on each offbeat; offbeat hats; accented 16th shakers; congas on root or fifth, a few syncopated hits per 2 bars. Organic tracks often need only one extra, tonal percussion part. Fills at 4- and 8-bar ends, sometimes displaced, or none ("Rej"). *Why:* the drums hold still while the melody changes. ([EDMProd][emh], [Attack/prog][apt], [Transition][tr], [Attack/Romance][amro])

**G3. Swing lightly; keep drums on the grid.** On Linn's scale (50% straight, 66% triplet): melodic 50–54%, since 54% loosens 16ths without sounding swung; deep, jazzy and organic 55–61% (Attack gives 50–60; MPC grooves 59 and 61). Linn finds random timing adds little once swing and dynamics are right; Myloops allows ±5–10 ms on arp and lead only. *Why:* the feel lives in accents on a fixed grid. ([Attack/Linn][lin], [Myloops][myc], Attack beats: [deep][bd1], [organic][bd2], [jazzy][bd3]; [EDMProd/deep][edh])

**G4. Add hand drums.** Darbuka in 8th slots (D doum, T tek, dot rest): `DT.TD.T.`, `DD.TD.T.` or `DT.T.D.T`. Sources swap the names maqsoum, baladi and maksum. *Why:* hand drums mark organic house. ([Wikipedia][wmq], [Tapadum][tap], [BLOND:ISH][bl])

### Arrangement

**A1. Build in 8-bar units and 16-bar blocks.** Ghost's grid: bars 1–16 kick, hat, texture; 17–32 bass and a percussion part; 33–48 kick out, pad open, lead fragments; 49–64 drop. Rotate a detail every 8 bars. Intros run 8 bars in single edits, 16–32 for clubs, 32–64 in 7-minute techno, and organic guides want 6–9-minute tracks; for radio, use 8–16-bar intros, 3.5–5.5 minutes, and an outro mirroring the intro ("Rej"). *Why:* DJs and dancers count in 8s. ([Ghost][gp], [Myloops/structure][myp], [Myloops][myc], [BLOND:ISH][bl], [Attack/Rej][arej])

**A2. Cut, climb, peak, late.** At the breakdown the level falls at once (kick and bass out), builds, and peaks at the drop, jumping in register. Place it in the last third (Caribou: 4.5 minutes into 6.5). Organic tracks lift gently instead. *Why:* drops raised skin conductance and brought intense pleasure. ([Solberg & Dibben][sd], [Attack/Caribou][acar], [BLOND:ISH][bl])

**A3. Let filters carry motion.** Low-pass ramps: pad 800 Hz→3.2 kHz over 8 bars; lead over 16–32; arp over 16; bass 600 Hz→1.2 kHz over 16. Filter the stripped beat low; bypass at the drop. *Why:* the notes stay put, so the tone moves, and listeners liked low-passed breakdowns. ([Ghost][gp], [Myloops][myc], [Attack/Caribou][acar], [Solberg & Dibben][sd])

**A4. Mark each change.** Before a drop: a 2–8-bar riser, longer each time; more reverb on the last clap; one silent beat; a crash and falling sweep on the downbeat. Organic style: a reverb wash, no riser. *Why:* risers and drum rolls were the moments listeners felt most. ([Ghost][gp], [Attack/Romance][amro], [Solberg & Dibben][sd], [Transition][tr])

### Sound

**S1. Pluck.** Bright saw plus a 4-voice detuned saw an octave down; short attack, fast decay; 12 dB low-pass on the same envelope; ~20% downsampling; delay, big reverb. *Why:* a snappy filter envelope suits heavy delay. ([MusicRadar][mrp])

**S2. Pad.** Saw, 4–5 voices detuned ~20–25%, plus a saw an octave down; gentle low-pass; attack 100–300 ms, release 0.5–1.5 s; cutoff LFO over 4–8 bars; 3–5 s reverb; bus high-passed near 140 Hz. *Why:* slow attack and moving cutoff keep a held chord alive. ([Myloops][myc], [Ghost][gp])

**S3. Bass and kick.** Sub: sine, low-pass 200 Hz, 200–300 ms decay, no sustain, mono. Mid: 2-voice saw plus a square an octave up at −12 dB, 100–800 Hz, 150 ms decay. Kick: click and fast pitch drop (melodic), or soft and round (organic). *Why:* the sub ducks deep; the mid stays heard. ([Myloops/bass][myb], [EDMProd][emh], [BLOND:ISH][bl])

**S4. Duduk-like reed.** A duduk acts like a clarinet with a low reed resonance, so its first harmonic dominates: a dark tone. Square or triangle; a low-pass that opens with loudness and tracks pitch below 100%; breath at the attack; vibrato near 5–7 Hz; a 10-note range; a tonic drone beneath. Start the cutoff near 2–3× pitch. *Why:* the fundamental gives darkness; breath and vibrato give life. ([Maugeais & Dalmont][dud], [SOS][sos], [Wikipedia][wdu], [ISMA][isma])

**S5. Oud-like pluck, and the mix.** Karplus–Strong string, hard plectrum click, slides (no frets); open strings D2–C4; paired strings suggest two voices a few cents apart (untested). Mix: slight detune, a little noise, some parts dry, short plate on drums. *Why:* Keinemusik chase a live feel with detune and noise; Böhmer keeps some parts dry. ([Wikipedia][wou], [Karplus–Strong][ks], [Keinemusik][kei], [PML/Böhmer][pbo], [Transition][tr])

## How house differs from lofi in a rule engine

**Carries over:** plan as data; critic and sameness check; ranged stations and patches; scale-governed colour (harmonic-minor V is already Phrygian dominant); least-movement voicing under the melody band; Melody v2's skeleton and two-thirds arch; one swing map; one standout trait.

**Inverts:**
- Functional grammar with secondary dominants and tritone subs → modal loops.
- ½–2 chords a bar → a chord per 2–4 bars; shapes gain entries like `'8x0.25'`.
- B avoids A's loop → one loop all track, varied by layers, filters, voicing.
- 54–66% swing, pocket lags, drag, ghost snares → 50–61% swing, drums on the grid, small jitter on arp and lead only.
- Bass locks to the kick → most bass families dodge the kick, and all duck under it.
- Rests on 9ths, 11ths, maj7s → chord tones and marked notes (♭2, raised 7th, Hijaz's 4th).
- Melody v2 fixes off-chord strong beats → a few appoggiaturas stay.
- 12-semitone band → about 16, top note once.
- Heavy tape and vinyl → light detune and noise.
- Never flat for 16 bars → layers may hold 32 bars if a detail changes every 8.

**New:** arrangement by layers, with the break routine as data; automation lanes (filter ramps, duck depth, reverb throws, risers, silences); generators for arps, bass families, stabs, drones and counter-melodies; Hijaz rules with cent offsets; hand drums; motif planting; a tuned kick; critic checks (drop after 60%, answering parts never overlap, top note once).

## A first station sketch

Caspian Night, retuned to the taste data, with Silk Road Dusk's duduk and hand drums. Comments mark new fields.

```js
{ id: 'caspian-night', genre: 'house', name: 'Caspian Night',
  music: {
    bpm: [104, 116], modes: { harmonicMinor: 3, phrygianDominant: 2, aeolian: 1 }, // new modes
    loops: { andalusian: 3, 'i-VI': 2, 'i-VI-III-VII': 2, 'I-bII': 2, 'i-V-VI-V': 1, 'v-VI-VII-i': 1, pedal: 1 }, // new
    shapes: { '8x0.25': 3, '8x0.5': 2, '16x0.25': 2 }, colour: { triad: 3, sus: 2, plain: 1 }, // new colours
    register: [48, 55], lead: [72, 77], swing: [0.51, 0.57],
    basslines: { offbeat: 3, rolling: 2, rootRhythm: 1, fifth: 1, pedal: 1 },
    keys: { pad: 3, stab: 1 }, comps: { hold: 3, offbeat: 1, anticipate: 1, dotted: 1 },
    leads: { oud: 3, duduk: 2, pluck: 2 }, arp: 0.6, counter: 0.5, drone: 0.4, // new
    kits: { soft: 2, click: 1 }, perc: { shaker: 1, darbuka: 0.5, conga: 0.3 },
    form: { intro: 16, groove: 16, lift: 16, break: 16, build: 8, drop: 32, outro: 8 }, // new
    intros: { drumsFirst: 3, filtered: 2, cold: 1 }, pump: { bass: [3, 6], pad: [1, 3] }, // dB per part
    ramps: [8, 32], risers: [2, 8], detune: [3, 8], tape: [0.05, 0.2], // bars, bars, cents
  },
}
```

**Form:** 112 bars, about 4:04 at 110 BPM, drop at bar 73 (64%).
- Intro 16: kick, hats, shaker, then a filtered glimpse of the motif.
- Groove 16: bass, clap and darbuka join.
- Lift 16: pad, arp or counter-melody, open hat.
- Break 16: kick and bass out; duduk or oud states the motif over a drone.
- Build 8: kick returns; a riser grows; one beat falls silent.
- Drop 32: full hook, answered by a second voice; one octave jump.
- Outro 8: drums and bass, mirroring the intro.
